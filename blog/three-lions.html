A Letter to the Three Lions

by everly · 22 May 2026 · 4 min read

I was two years old the first time I saw England play. I don't remember it. But my dad says I sat in front of the telly with my arms folded and watched the whole thing without blinking — which apparently I still do, and which my wife finds extremely irritating.

Thirty-three years of this. Thirty-three years of tournaments and near-misses and penalty shootouts I can still feel in my stomach. Thirty-three years of believing, which is — if I'm honest — a form of madness. Benign, communal, English madness. But madness nonetheless.

The squad came out today. I read it standing in the hallway, jacket still on, like I was expecting to find something between the lines. Some hidden clue about how this one ends.

the one before

I'm writing this now because I know what happens to the feeling.

Right now it's clean. The hope hasn't curdled yet. Bellingham hasn't miscontrolled it in the box. The penalty hasn't been saved. It's the 22nd of May and anything still feels possible.

I want to keep this version. The one before.

So. A letter. Written today, to be opened when it's over. To the Three Lions. To all twenty-six of them boarding a plane to North America carrying the usual impossible weight.

a message from chan man

dear lads,

22 may 2026

You'll know by now how it ended. I don't, yet. That's the strange thing about writing to you like this — you're somewhere in my future and I'm here in your past.

Kane is going to his third World Cup. He's still here. I've moved house twice since his debut. He keeps turning up, reliable as gravity, slightly better than the tournament deserves.

Wednesday 17th June. Nine o'clock. Dallas. Croatia. Again. My mate Dave already sent a voice note — forty-five seconds of heavy breathing, then "it's Croatia again," and he hung up. We'll be nervous from about four in the afternoon. We'll pretend we aren't.

I'm thirty-five. I'm old enough to know better than to tell you this is the year.

But here's what I do know: it still matters. The flags in the windows. The group chats. My dad texting "Kane's going again" at half past ten like I might have missed it. Twenty million people stopping what they're doing on a Tuesday night in June to watch football in Massachusetts.

That is not a small thing.

So go. Play. Give us the summer we need. Know that we were with you — all the way to the edge of whatever this becomes.

Yours,
a 35-year-old man who has been doing this since he was two. and has absolutely no intention of stopping.
